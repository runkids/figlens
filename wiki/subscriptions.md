# Change subscriptions

`watch subscribe|evaluate|list|unsubscribe|forget` (`src/workspace/changes/subscriptions.rs`; schema 6, after the watch schema 5) keeps durable subscriptions: one consumer follows one visible source, scope or exact node (its kind in the published index is the anchor: no layer is `target_missing`, two kinds are `identity_uncertain`, a later other kind reads as `identity_uncertain`; never a same-name match). Each holds its own checkpoint, bound to its id and `FILTER_VERSION`, starting at the newest revision its view sees; `changes`/`ack` with `subscriptionId` refuse `consumer`, `sourceId` and `scope`. A node subscription reads revisions whose changed rows name the node or have it as top-level frame, or whose stored impact lists the node, a hit inside it as top-level frame on either side, or is incomplete (schema 7), plus baselines, incomplete details and incomplete comparisons (`coverage.complete` not true), since either may hide its change (a deeper node's descendants are not matched; a top-level frame's are only through impact hits it holds). The subscribe reply is read inside its write, so it never describes a source moved out of view meanwhile. `owner` marks a temporary subscription removed by `unsubscribe --consumer C --owner O`; one consumer cannot hold the same target under two owners or lifetimes, and a repeat reports `initialPosition: existing`. `forget` ignores sources removed since a view was written. Raw consumer rows now record the visible sources they were written under; `forget` removes only rows whose sources the caller sees all of, and legacy rows only for a caller who sees every source (else counted in `keptUnknownOwnership`). A consumer holds at most 64 subscriptions and raw checkpoints, a workspace 4096, checked in the writing transaction: past that a new one is `workspace_busy` naming list, unsubscribe and forget; nothing is evicted. Visibility is resolved inside each transaction; a subscription on a hidden source reads as unknown. Removing a source deletes its subscriptions.

A `query` subscription (`src/workspace/changes/queries.rs`) saves one workspace search of one source's scope (default `0:0`, hidden and internal layers excluded, `type` sorted): its target is the search's own normalized phrase, terms and types, so spellings search normalizes alike are one subscription. Its events are evaluations in `query_evaluations` (cascade-deleted with the subscription), not revisions. A new subscribe stores the first after its own write (`initialPosition: initial_evaluation`, a failed one reported as `unavailable` with an evaluate step; a repeat evaluates nothing); `watch evaluate` stores the next. `type` without `query` is refused. Each runs `query::search` with `limit` 50 outside any write, reading the publication before and after it (unequal is `pending: newer_publication`), and publishes inside one IMMEDIATE write only when the subscription, its target and that publication still hold; the same generation, membership and ranking is `current`, writing nothing, and a new generation whose snapshot, coverage, ranking and window are unchanged only rebinds the newest evaluation to it (no event, nothing evicted). A stale, unreadable, incompatible or too-large index is `unavailable: search_failed` with the search's recovery; a removed or hidden subscription is unknown; a target search now normalizes differently is `unavailable: query_changed` (resubscribe). The runner's hook `changes::evaluate_published(source, scope, from, cancel)` works in bounded turns: one call runs at most one canonical search that a subscription is due for (no evaluation and no noted failure at the scope's publication; indexed by `subscriptions_query`) and one short write recording it for at most `FANOUT` (32) of them, a savepoint each. The returned in-memory `Continuation` keeps the publication, position and that search's outcome, so later turns record the rest without searching again; at another publication it restarts from the first search, and `None` discovers due work. `cancel` interrupts the search in progress (SQLite through its interrupt handle, and the load, posting and ranking loops through `query::search_with`, which plain `search` skips); a cancelled turn writes and notes nothing, its write checked again after it begins and before commit. Its write waits at most 250 ms for another writer, then fails `workspace_busy` for the runner to retry. A failed search or record is noted on each subscription (`eval_failure`, at most 4 KiB, keyed to the publication, written inside the write only while the source is visible and its scope still published there; a newer publication leaves it `pending` and the turn restarts, a removed or hidden subscription is unknown), so it is not rerun before the next publication or `watch evaluate`, and `changes` reports it as its stored state with its reason; `watch evaluate` notes its own failures at the publication it read. A watch runner calls `evaluate_published` for every scope it serves after each publication ([watch](watch.md)). A hidden source loads nothing. `changes` reads them, admitting rows by the actual SQL lengths of the columns it emits before copying or parsing any (a first row past `PAGE_BYTES` fails `response_budget_exceeded` and a damaged first row `source_unreadable` (`InvalidData`), each with an ack-past recovery; a damaged later row ends the page before it), and reports `current`, `current_for_index` (the file's size or mtime differs from the published snapshot, or it is gone: refresh, then evaluate), `pending` with an evaluate step, or `unavailable`, with the view's `tracking`; it never writes. Causes: `source_changed` only for another snapshot, `index_rebuilt` for the same bytes, `coverage_changed`, `ranking_changed`, `corpus_changed` (the source's membership changed, as a relocation does; a set's also covers a member's publication and shared ranking statistics, below). `enteredTopK` and `leftTopK` compare the two top-50 windows as exact refs in the new and the old snapshot (`snapshotId`, `fromSnapshotId`), `rankChanged` lists the same exact refs in the new snapshot with `from` and `to` positions (a set's nodeIds repeat across sources), null when the rankings differ (`rankComparable: false`), nodes are compared by `sourceId`, which relocate keeps (membership only fences the computation; remove deletes a single-source subscription), and `delta.from` names the evaluation compared, since publications between evaluations are coalesced; `total` is set only when nothing ranked past the window and coverage is complete. Each keeps 16; pruning raises `pruned_seq`, which the cursor receipt binds, so a checkpoint or cursor behind it is `event_gap`. One evaluation may take half of `PAGE_BYTES`, so it always fits a page with the reply's own fields (else `unavailable: evaluation_too_large`, nothing written); the workspace's evaluation bytes are capped at 8 MiB and the database's used pages (page count less freelist, so rows deleted by unsubscribe make room) at `DISK_CAP`, both checked before the insert: past that is `workspace_busy`. A relocation always draws a new random membership (`registry.rs` relocate), so a source moved away and back during a search never matches the membership read before it.

A query subscription may instead hold a fixed source set (schema 8, `V8`): `sources` is `visible` (what the caller sees at subscribe, resolved once inside the write) or comma-separated sourceIds each resolved as `sourceId` is (the list is refused past 10 names or `SET_BYTES` (4 KiB) before any is resolved; CLI `--namespace`, `--key` and `--definition-*` without `--library` are refused), stored sorted and deduplicated in `subscriptions.sources` with `source_id` '*' (`subscriptions::SET`, never a real id: `*` as input is refused), at most `query::QUERY_SOURCES` (10). A source added later is never enrolled. Its search passes the set as `only`; or it saves a library question instead (`library {kind, namespace, key, definition}`, versioned by `LIBRARY_VERSION` inside kind `query`, no `type`): `library::library_in` over the set, the exact definition checked at subscribe (its source must be a member, its snapshot the published one) and then followed by nodeId in that source's current snapshot. Its evaluation stores the members' `[sourceId, gen, membership, snapshot]` (gen 0, `snapshot_id` empty), its refs `[sourceId, nodeId]` (library: plus `definitionMatch`); `delta` adds `sources`, `fromSources` and `changedSourceIds`, a library one `definitionMatchChanged` (uses whose match moved) with `rankComparable` false, and `coverage.transitive` is always `not_followed`: a library's own library dependencies are never walked, so a change there reaches no use's match; a library event's `delta.notFollowedSourceIds` makes that visible, naming the changed members that are not the definition's source and hold no use in either window. Causes: `source_changed` when any member's snapshot changed, else `index_rebuilt`; `corpus_changed` when a member gained or lost its publication or changed membership, or a ranked search of several members changed (their shared statistics). The runner's due query treats a set as due at a member's publication when its newest evaluation's members differ, so each member's turn evaluates it once, under the same `FANOUT`, byte and disk caps; it considers only sets whose every member the runner sees. Reads never persist: one rule (`subscriptions::sees_set`, `seen` in SQL) sees a set when the caller sees every member still registered, else it reads as unknown in `list`, `changes`, `evaluate` and `unsubscribe` (`list --subscription`/`subscriptionId` reads only that row by its key under this rule, empty when unknown); `describe` lists only registered members, and a definition whose source was removed with its ids null. A stored set that is not a JSON array of at most 10 text ids within `SET_BYTES`, or holds any JSON escape (SQLite's JSON and serde disagree on some, such as a lone surrogate), is unreadable: it reads as `disabled` `{reason: sources_unreadable}` with `sources` null, never due, and only a caller who sees every source sees or unsubscribes it (forget's rule for unknown ownership). Only `registry::remove` of a member, inside its authorized write, sets the sticky `disabled` (`{reason: source_removed}`, naming no member a caller may never have seen) and deletes the set's evaluations (single-source subscriptions are still deleted); `changes` and `evaluate` then report `disabled` with no evaluations, `ack` is refused, and a subscribe meeting the disabled row is refused with an unsubscribe recovery, so an old cursor never becomes a fresh baseline. A missing or locked file is `current_for_index`/`unavailable`, never disabled; relocate keeps members (next evaluation `corpus_changed`), and no relocation outside the workspace exists to disable on.

Hosts: CLI `watch subscribe|evaluate|list|unsubscribe|forget` and `changes|watch ack --subscription`, MCP `watch` actions of the same names with `subscriptionId`, `query`, `type`, `sources` and `library` (CLI `--sources`, `--library` with `--namespace`, `--key` and `--definition-source|-snapshot|-node`); Pi and OMP use the shared MCP tools; OpenCode's CLI path maps them through `figlens_watch` and `figlens_changes`, asking edit on the workspace for every action but `list` and `status`, and `start` also read on its source. `evaluate` runs on the subscription's own source or set, so every host refuses a `sourceId`/`--source` on it before running anything; OpenCode asks read on exactly the sources one `watch list --subscription` names, plus a library question's definition source (every registered source when that lookup finds nothing or the definition source is unlisted or null), before an `evaluate` or a `changes` with `subscriptionId`, and a query or library `subscribe` asks it on the sources it names and the definition's (every source for `visible`), which the binary reads. The served skill's Following changes section routes subscribe after the baseline, unsubscribe to stop following (never `untrack` unless asked) and `owner` only for task-temporary subscriptions. Revision recording and `ack` semantics are in [changes](changes.md).
