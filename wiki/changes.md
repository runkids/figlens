# Change tracking

`index track` and `index untrack` (`src/workspace/jobs.rs`) record a tracked scope's revisions as it is published, compared with the shared [diff](diff.md) engine (#92). Generations, jobs and publication fences are in [index](index-jobs.md).

## Tracking

A build of a tracked scope (a `tracking` row not `disabled`) also writes its records' diff facets and the generation's facet layout and selected-node context (`facet_sets`), in the same fenced batches as records; they are deleted with their generation. An untracked build writes no facets. `index track` (CLI `index track --scope N [--source S]`, MCP `index` action `track`) needs the scope published: it sets the row `baseline_pending`, bumps its `epoch` and submits a `rebuild` of that scope, so the baseline never reuses an unchanged snapshot; a scope already tracked keeps its epoch and builds nothing unless its baseline is still pending, which builds it again. Only a rebuild that reads tracking on can record the baseline: one not yet past its `hash` stage (the worker reads tracking at parse), or one admitted after the pending baseline was enabled (job `seq` above the row's `enabled_seq`, the largest job `seq` at `track`'s commit; never the clock), which a repeated `track` joins. While any other build of the scope runs with a live owner (a dead owner's is taken over as the baseline rebuild), `track` is refused `workspace_busy` before it changes anything: joining would publish a generation without facets. A `track` requestId used by another request is refused before tracking changes; its own retry replays the job without changing tracking, so it never re-tracks after `untrack`; one that found the scope already tracked keeps its id too (a `requests` row with no job, the newest `KEPT` kept) and replays as no job. An active tracked scope deleted from the file refreshes empty and records its removal; other missing ones are refused. `index untrack` sets `disabled` and bumps the epoch; stored revisions stay. Removing a source deletes its tracking and revisions with it; a relocate keeps the id and the history.

## Revisions

Before that transaction, an actively tracked scope's previous and new generations are compared from the database in one read transaction (`diff::revision`, the same `compare` as `figlens diff`, which also computes the revision's [impact](diff.md)), outside any write. Inside it, the generation is compared only if the `tracking` row is as read (same state and `epoch`) and it replaces the generation it was compared with; an active scope whose `epoch` or previous generation changed meanwhile records a `baseline` instead (`tracking_changed`, `previous_changed`), and a disabled one or a generation without facets publishes with no revision. `baseline_pending` writes a `baseline` revision (`reason: tracking_started`) and becomes `active`. A previous generation without facets, or with another `facet_version`, is a `baseline` too (`previous_untracked`, `facet_version_changed`), never compared, and so is a scope published again after eviction left it no published generation (`previous_unpublished`): never a silent gap. A comparison that fails (unreadable facets, I/O) still publishes, with a `changes` revision whose coverage is partial with `comparison_failed` and detail `truncated`, never an empty one. A revision stores exact counts per change kind and facet in `summary` and at most 10,000 change rows or 4 MiB (`detail: truncated` past either, or when the rows would pass `DISK_CAP` in used pages). Schema 7 records the impact with it, in the same transaction: `revisions.impact` is the summary (`complete`, `reasons` with counts, `listed`: the count per class of the hits it keeps, never a total), null for a baseline and for a revision recorded before schema 7 (never read as an empty complete one), and `revision_impacts` keeps the hits in order, each with its top-level frame on the old and new side (`diff::place`, from the pair's id maps, paid first; a hit it cannot place is `frames_unknown`; the frames are stored, not listed, and their bytes count), sharing the revision's 4 MiB and `DISK_CAP` preflight with its rows: the summary's largest form is reserved first (the last rows give way to it, and the detail is `truncated`), and its actual length counts in `detail_bytes`; each hit pays its copy before it is built, then its entry's exact serialized bytes (JSON escaping included, through a capped counting writer); the first that does not fit ends the list, and the omitted count is `impact_truncated` (the detail is `truncated`), kept even with zero hits. A failed comparison's impact is incomplete with `comparison_failed`. An incomplete impact is final, not pending: nothing fills or retries it later, so there is no separate impact-ready event, and a reader sees a revision and its impact together or not at all. A workspace-wide `revision_seq` in `meta` orders revisions, never the clock; ids are 16 random bytes. Retention works on whole groups in the same transaction: past 1,000 revisions per source and scope the oldest are deleted, and past 16 MiB of change rows and hits the oldest keep only their summaries (`detail: expired`); both detail tables are deleted together, as on source removal. The replaced generation's facets are dropped once its revision commits.

## Reading changes

`changes` (`src/workspace/changes.rs`; CLI `figlens changes <select>`, MCP `changes`, read-only) never writes or builds. It reads one SQLite read transaction over the visible sources only (a `sourceId` filter must be visible; hidden sources are neither listed nor counted), optionally one `scope`. A poll pages revision groups oldest first (limit 1-50, default 20, at most 256 KiB) after `cursor`, else after `consumer`'s checkpoint, else from the oldest kept, and lists `tracking` (at most 100, else `trackingTruncated`; each tracked scope's `state`, `lastRevisionId`, `lastCheckedAtMs` and `refreshRequired` from the file's stamp against its published snapshot) apart from `monitor` ([watching](watch.md)). `revision` pages that revision's stored rows, then its stored impact hits, instead (limit 1-500 entries, default 100), with `detail` explicit; on a workspace before schema 7 it lists `impact` null and no hits, never upgrading. A node filter also matches a revision whose stored hits name the node or hold it as their old or new top-level frame, or whose impact is incomplete (it cannot rule the node out). A summary's `listed` counts what was recorded: an expired detail keeps it, with no hits. A workspace before schema 4 has nothing recorded.

Revision ids are the 16 random bytes in hex. A cursor is `c1.` (poll: id and receipt) or `d2.` (detail: id, section and position; change rows are section 0, then impact hits section 1, each entry naming its `entry`; a `d1.` cursor, issued before impact, is read as a change row position) and the payload plus the first 16 bytes of an HMAC-SHA256 keyed by `meta.cursor_secret` over the incarnation, the filters, the hash of the visible source ids and the tag, all in hex, its length checked before decoding: another workspace, incarnation, filter or visibility is `cursor_stale`, and the internal `seq` is never exposed. Retention records, per source and scope in `meta` (`pruned`, source, scope), the newest deleted `seq` and the `revision_seq` of the publication that deleted it. A `c1.` cursor carries a receipt, a keyed digest of the view's marks when issued, never a seq; the marks are filtered to its sources and scope in SQL and streamed within 1 MiB (64 bytes plus the key each; past it `query_work_limit` names a narrower `sourceId` or scope, never a receipt of part of them): a hidden source's publication leaves a visible cursor byte-identical. A poll cursor whose revision is gone, or whose receipt is not the current one while its position is behind the view's newest mark, is `event_gap` (a new deletion in one group can report a gap to a reader behind another group's older mark: it repeats, never skips). The recovery repeats the filters with the oldest cursor (a zero id and the current receipt: every revision still kept, of every group in the view; the gap stays reported, never recovered), so its pages read on instead of into the gap again. A checkpoint keeps the private `revision_seq` of its ack as `consumers.floor`, and is behind a gap when a mark deleted after that floor lies past its position. A detail `revision` that is gone is `event_gap` too when the view has any mark, else `invalid_arguments`. Removing a source deletes its `pruned` marks and the checkpoints of filters naming it.

`watch ack` (CLI `figlens watch ack <select> --consumer C --cursor X`, MCP `watch` action `ack`) validates the cursor for the same filters and refuses a cursor that missed a deletion as `event_gap`, writing nothing, else stores `consumers.after_seq` only forward and the floor at now; a current cursor before a checkpoint that is behind an unaccepted deletion moves the checkpoint back to it instead (repeats, never skips), keyed by consumer and view (filters and the visible-source hash, so a narrower view never inherits progress over sources it cannot see), in one IMMEDIATE transaction. Otherwise a retry or an older cursor leaves the position, so the checkpoint is monotone except for that recovery rewind; `advanced` is true only for visible forward progress, never a floor refresh or a rewind; the reply carries a cursor at the checkpoint held, or null when retention deleted that revision. A view changes when a source is added or removed, so its consumers then read from the oldest kept again (repeats, never skips); checkpoints of a view no longer used stay until their source is removed (a follow-up bounds them).

Subscriptions and checkpoint bounds are in [subscriptions](subscriptions.md).
